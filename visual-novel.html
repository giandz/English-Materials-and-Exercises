✍️ How a scene is written

The scene is the JSON in this page’s #vnScript: a backdrop, a cast of house characters (as saved by the poser) and a list of lines. Each line names who speaks and what they say, and can also set where they stand (left, center, right), the shot (full, medium, closeup), a pose (any pose preset, including the emotion ones such as em_joy), a face (emotion) or other details (look). stage brings other characters in, moves them, or takes them away with null. A line’s place, shot, pose and face are how the speaker acts it: when the line has been said, they slowly go back to their resting state (where they first came on, with their own pose and face). Use stage to change that state for good; look always stays.

A character can change pose or face in the middle of a line: put a cue in the text where it should happen, such as "Music? {pose:em_surprise} Wow! {emotion:joy} I love music." A cue is {pose:…}, {emotion:…} or both, {pose:em_joy emotion:sadness}. It is not shown or said; it happens when the text reaches it, and lasts until the line ends.

Pose changes are eased with the keyframe editor’s easings. "easing" (an easing id such as quad, sine, back, elastic or bounce, or {"out":…, "in":…} for the two ends) and "moveTime" (seconds) set them for the scene, and any line can set its own for the moves it starts.

Kinds of line: a line with no "who" is narration, in the box with no name. "insert": true shows the text as a caption over the backdrop (for a new place or time passing), and "insert": "black" on a black card. "think": true shows a character’s thoughts: their lips don’t move and everything else turns grey. "shout": true makes each letter shake as it appears, then settle, and "whisper": true makes the text smaller and softer; for part of a line, wrap it in the text: "{shout}Hi, Ana!{/shout} Sorry I'm late." or "Shh! {whisper}He can hear you!{/whisper}". "past": true turns the picture sepia for memories. "background" changes the backdrop from that line on; a character who left with null comes back where they were with {}.

Questions branch the conversation. Give a line an "id" so others can "goto" it, and end the scene with "end": true. "choices" shows buttons, each with its "goto". "input" asks for a typed answer and compares it with each branch’s "rubric" (one answer or a list): with "check": "match" the answer must be one of them (ignoring case, extra spaces and end punctuation); with "check": "closeness" each branch scores how close the answer is (the gap fill’s Damerau-Levenshtein accuracy, 0–100), and the closest branch that reaches its "min" wins, so the same rubric can lead to a “perfect” branch at 95 and a “nearly” branch at 65. "else" is where an answer that fits no branch goes. "save": "name" keeps the answer for later lines as {$name}.

Keys: Space / Enter / → next (or finish the line), ← back, Home start again, 1–9 pick a choice.
