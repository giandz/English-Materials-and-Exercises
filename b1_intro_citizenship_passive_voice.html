🏛️ USCIS office — the N-400 interview
Conversation
👨‍💼
Officer
Good morning. Please have a seat. Today, you will be asked some questions about your application.
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
Good morning. I'm a little nervous.
👨‍💼
Officer
Don't worry. First, you are required to raise your right hand and take an oath.
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
Okay, I'm ready.
👨‍💼
Officer
Thank you. Now, your fingerprints were already taken at your biometrics appointment, correct?
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
Yes, they were taken last month.
👨‍💼
Officer
Good. You are also expected to answer some questions about your background. Have you ever been arrested?
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
No, never.
👨‍💼
Officer
Great. A valid photo ID is needed to confirm your identity. Do you have it?
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
Yes, here is my green card.
👨‍💼
Officer
Perfect. Now we'll do the civics test. You are required to answer six out of ten questions correctly.
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
I studied a lot for this!
👨‍💼
Officer
I'm sure you'll do well. After the interview, you will be notified by mail if you are approved.
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
And then the oath of allegiance?
👨‍💼
Officer
Yes, if everything is approved, you will be invited to a naturalization ceremony, where the oath is taken.
🙋‍♀️
Ms. Gomez
Thank you so much for explaining everything.
👨‍💼
Officer
You're welcome. Good luck!

Quick reference
be + past participle (+ to + infinitive) is common in formal, legal, and procedural English: you are required to..., you will be asked to..., documents are needed to.... It focuses on the rule or process, not on who is enforcing it.
Key vocabulary
application
the official form you submit (e.g. Form N-400)
green card
ID card showing you are a permanent resident
fingerprints / biometrics appointment
when your fingerprints and photo are taken
background check
a review of your history and records
civics test
questions about U.S. history and government
oath of allegiance
a formal promise of loyalty to the U.S.
naturalization ceremony
the event where you officially become a citizen
approved
accepted / given a positive decision
