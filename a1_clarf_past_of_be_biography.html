Was and Were — Clarification

Two forms, one verb — and the special case of born.

To talk about a life — where someone was born, what their family did, how things were — you need the past of be. There are only two forms: was and were.
When does it happen?
be describes a state — who or what someone was. It is not an action. That is why was and were are used for ages, jobs, places and feelings in the past.
Only two forms
Iwas
he / she / itwas
youwere
we / theywere
💡 The odd one out is I was. Everywhere else, singular → was, plural → were.

There it is. The negative adds not straight after was/were (wasn’t, weren’t). The question moves was/were in front of the subject. You never need did with be.
Born: a special case
be born is always passive. You cannot “born” — you were born.
✗ I born in Chile.   ✗ I am born in 1998.   ✓ I was born in Chile in 1998.
