Causative Get and Have — Quick Reference

Talking about things someone else does for you

The idea: we use have or get something done when someone else does a job for us — we arrange it, but we don't do it ourselves.
have + object + past participle
have + object + past participle
More formal and neutral, often planned in advance.

I had the sofa delivered.
She had her windows cleaned.
get + object + past participle
get + object + past participle
More informal. Similar meaning — often a bit more effort involved.

I got the kettle fixed.
She got her shelves installed.
Key point: both mean you did not do it yourself — someone did it for you. The object always goes right after have/get, and the past participle comes last.
Common mistakes
✗I had delivered the sofa.
✓I had the sofa delivered.
✗She got fixed her car.
✓She got her car fixed.
✗I have cut my hair. (this means YOU cut it)
✓I have my hair cut. (someone else cuts it for you)

Other forms

Negative: I haven't had my hair cut yet.  ·  We didn't get the windows cleaned this month.
Continuous: We're having the kitchen renovated.  ·  I'm getting my curtains fitted.
