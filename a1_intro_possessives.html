Whose Is It?

A short conversation about clothes and who they belong to.

Before you read

Read these three questions first, then look for the answers in the conversation.

🏫 two friends, outside school
Conversation
👨‍🦱
Sam
Hi Ana! I like your jacket. Is it new?
🙋
Ana
Thanks! Yes, it's new. Is that your hat?
👨‍🦱
Sam
No, it's not my hat — it's my brother's hat. He let me borrow it.
🙋
Ana
It looks great on you! Whose shoes are those?
👨‍🦱
Sam
These are my shoes. I bought them last week.
🙋
Ana
Nice! Look, that's Leo over there. Is that his jacket?
👨‍🦱
Sam
Yes, that's Leo's jacket. He wears it every day.
🙋
Ana
And that's Mia next to him — is that her scarf?
👨‍🦱
Sam
Yes, it's her scarf. It's very colorful.
🙋
Ana
Look at Tom and Lily. Are those their backpacks?
👨‍🦱
Sam
Yes, those are their backpacks. They always match!
🙋
Ana
Oh, look — a dog! It's wearing a little sweater.
👨‍🦱
Sam
Ha! Its owner must really love it.
🙋
Ana
Is this backpack yours or mine? I can't remember.
👨‍🦱
Sam
It's yours! Mine is at home.
🙋
Ana
Oh right! Thanks. Our class starts soon — let's go!
👨‍🦱
Sam
Good idea. See you there!

Quick reference
my, your, his, her, its, our, their go before a noun to show who it belongs to: my hat, her scarf. Add 's to a name to show possession: Leo's jacket.
