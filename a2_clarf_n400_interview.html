Have you ever + past participle …? = at any time in your life, in any country. Have you ever been arrested? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t. / No, never.

The short answer uses the first word of the question. See the table below.

Always tell the truth. Listen to the whole question. Then answer. Some questions need “yes” and some need “no”.

Small talk: give a short, friendly answer. The officer listens to your English from the first “hello”.

How long …? for + a number of years (for six years) · since + the year it started (since 2020).

More small talk questions

The officer can ask about many things before the interview starts. Tap a question to hear it. The green line is one good answer.

Answer + one more thing. Not only “Yes.” Say a little more: “Yes, I drove. My son is waiting in the car.”

“…, isn’t it?” The officer thinks you agree. Answer with the same verb: It’s cold today, isn’t it? — Yes, it is!

“Sorry to keep you waiting.” It is not a question. Say: That’s OK. / No problem.

Small talk is in the past or the present. How did you get here? (this morning) · How are you feeling? (now)

Hard words: “What does … mean?”

The officer can ask you what a word means. Use easy words to answer.

The tests

Reading: the officer shows you a sentence. You read it out loud. You need to read one sentence correctly. You have three chances.

Writing: the officer says a sentence. You write it. You need to write one sentence correctly. You have three chances.

Civics: the officer asks questions about U.S. history and government. You answer by speaking. Did you send your N-400 before October 20, 2025? Up to 10 questions; you need 6 correct. On or after that date? Up to 20 questions; you need 12 correct.
