Present Perfect: Life Experiences B1 · Intermediate

We use the present perfect to talk about experiences — things that have (or haven't) happened at some point in a person's life. We don't say exactly when.

Key idea: the exact time isn't important — what matters is that the experience is now part of the person's life story, connected to the present moment.
Affirmative, negative & question forms
Subject + have/has + past participle (+ object)
  • I have ridden a horse.
  • She has already seen the Northern Lights.
  • We've tried sushi a few times.
Subject + haven't/hasn't + past participle (+ object)
  • I haven't eaten sushi in my life.
  • He hasn't run a marathon yet.
  • They haven't been abroad.
Have/Has + subject + (ever) + past participle (+ object)?
  • Have you ever been to Japan?
  • Has she tried scuba diving?
  • Have they met a celebrity?
Subject have/has Past participle Object Negation have/has (question) ever/never/already/yet
Adverb placement: ever and never go before the past participle (Have you ever been…? / I've never tried…). Already usually goes before the past participle too (I've already seen…). Yet goes at the end of negatives and questions (I haven't been yet. / Have you finished yet?).

Common mistakes
✗I have seen the Northern Lights two years ago.
✓I saw the Northern Lights two years ago.
✗Have you ever ran a marathon?
✓Have you ever run a marathon?
✗She has never been in Japan.
✓She has never been to Japan.
✗I have went to Brazil.
✓I have been to Brazil.
✗Did you ever try sushi? (about your whole life)
✓Have you ever tried sushi?
