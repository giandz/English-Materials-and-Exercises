What's Your Neighborhood Like? A1 / A2

Read the conversation between Anna and Ben. Notice how Anna describes her new neighborhood.

📍 Ben is asking Anna about her new neighborhood
there is / are (+ n't) determiner (many/much/a lot of…) is/are (question)
🧔

Ben

Hey! What's your new neighborhood like?
👩

Anna

It's nice, but there's a lot of traffic.
🧔

Ben

Really? Are there many parks?
👩

Anna

Yes, there are a few parks nearby, but there aren't many restaurants.
🧔

Ben

Is there much crime?
👩

Anna

No, there isn't much crime. It's very safe.
🧔

Ben

That's good! Is there a supermarket nearby?
👩

Anna

Yes, there's one supermarket, but there isn't a big shopping center.
🧔

Ben

Sounds quiet. Is there a lot of noise at night?
👩

Anna

No, there isn't much noise. It's peaceful.
Notice: Anna uses there is / there are to say what exists in a place. With words like traffic, crime, and noise (no plural — noncount nouns) she uses is / much / a lot of. With words like parks and restaurants (plural — count nouns) she uses are / many / a few. Let's look at this more closely.
