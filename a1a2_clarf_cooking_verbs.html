Cooking Verbs — Clarification

Three groups of cooking verbs, and how to use them in a recipe.

Cooking verbs fall into three groups: what you do before cooking, how you heat the food, and how you combine the ingredients.
The three groups
preparing 🔪
chopcut food into small pieces
cutdivide food with a knife
peelremove the skin of a fruit or vegetable
gratecut into small pieces with a grater
heating 🔥
boilcook in very hot, bubbling water
simmercook gently, with small bubbles
bakecook in the oven
frycook in hot oil in a pan
combining 🥄
mixput two or more things together
stirmove a spoon around in food or liquid
addput something into the food
pourmake a liquid flow into a container
Recipes use the imperative
A recipe gives instructions, so it uses the imperative — the plain verb with no subject. Not You boil the water, just Boil the water.

Boil the water in a big pot.

Chop the onion and the carrots.

Add a little salt and pepper.

Do not boil the soup — simmer it.

Putting the steps in order
Sequence words tell the cook what comes when: First · Then · Next · After that · Finally. They go at the start, followed by a comma.

First, peel the potatoes.

Then, boil them for 20 minutes.

Finally, add butter and salt.
