Quantifiers — Quick Reference

Talking about how much or how many of something there is

The idea: some words go with things you can count (eggs) and others go with things you can't count (milk). The color scale below goes from none (red) to all (green) — the same colors used for the level tag at the top of every lesson.
Asking the question

How much + uncountable noun: How much milk do we have?

How many + countable noun (plural): How many eggs do we have?

Countable — eggs, apples, friends…
0%no eggs
25%a few eggs
50%some eggs
75%many eggs
90%most eggs
100%all eggs
Uncountable — milk, sugar, time…
0%no milk
25%a little milk
50%some milk
75%a lot of milk
90%most milk
100%all milk
Some and any

Use some in positive sentences: We have some bread.

Use any in questions and negatives: Do we have any bread? / We don't have any bread.

Common mistakes
✗How much apples do you have?
✓How many apples do you have?
✗How many water do you drink?
✓How much water do you drink?
✗I have much friends.
✓I have many friends.
✗There is a few water.
✓There is a little water.
✗I don't have some money.
✓I don't have any money.
