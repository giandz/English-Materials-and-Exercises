Wearing, Carrying, Holding — Quick Reference

Saying what people have on, and what they have with them, right now

The idea: to describe a person we can see now, we use am / is / are + verb-ing. Three verbs do most of the work: wearing for clothes, carrying for things you take with you, and holding for things in your hand.
wearing
It is on your body: clothes, shoes, hats, glasses, a watch, jewellery.

She is wearing a red coat.
She is wearing a hat and glasses.
carrying
You take it with you, in your hand, on your back or in your arms: bags, boxes, luggage.

He is carrying a suitcase.
He is carrying a backpack.
holding
It is in your hand. You are not taking it anywhere: a phone, a cup, an umbrella.

She is holding a cup.
She is holding a phone.
Key point: ask yourself where the thing is. On the body → wearing. Going with the person (a bag, a box, a suitcase) → carrying. Just in the hand → holding. Small things in the hand are usually holding: we say holding a phone, not carrying a phone, when it is in your hand.
Common mistakes
✗He is using a blue coat.
✓He is wearing a blue coat. (clothes: wear, not use)
✗She wearing a red dress.
✓She is wearing a red dress. (don't forget am / is / are)
✗He is wear a hat.
✓He is wearing a hat. (the verb needs -ing)
✗She is wearing an umbrella.
✓She is holding an umbrella. (it is in her hand, not on her body)

The verb with -ing never changes. Only am / is / are changes: it takes not in a negative, and it moves in front of the subject in a question.
Other forms
Short forms: He's wearing jeans.  •  They're carrying boxes.  •  I'm holding my keys.
Negative: She isn't wearing a hat.  •  I'm not carrying anything.
Question: Is he holding a phone? — Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
Wh- question: What is she wearing?  •  What are they carrying?
Spelling: wear → wearing  •  carry → carrying (keep the y)  •  hold → holding
