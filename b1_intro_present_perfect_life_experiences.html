Talking About Life Experiences B1

Read the conversation between Maya and Leo. Notice how they talk about things they have and haven't done.

☕ Two old friends catching up over coffee
have / has (+ n't) past participle ever / never / already / yet
🧑‍🦱

Maya

Have you ever been to Japan?
🧔

Leo

No, I haven't been there yet, but I've always wanted to go.
🧑‍🦱

Maya

I've never eaten sushi in my life either, believe it or not!
🧔

Leo

Really? I've already tried sushi twice this year.
🧑‍🦱

Maya

What about adventure sports? Have you ever jumped from a plane?
🧔

Leo

Yes, I have! I went skydiving last summer. It was terrifying.
🧑‍🦱

Maya

Wow. I've ridden a horse, but I've never jumped from a plane.
🧔

Leo

Have you ever seen the Northern Lights?
🧑‍🦱

Maya

I've already seen the Aurora Borealis — in Iceland, two years ago.
🧔

Leo

I haven't seen it yet. It's still on my list.
🧑‍🦱

Maya

Have you ever run a marathon?
🧔

Leo

I haven't run a full marathon, but I've run a half marathon twice.
🧑‍🦱

Maya

That still counts! Life experiences don't have to be extreme.
🧔

Leo

True. I've broken a bone once, though — does that count as an "experience"?
🧑‍🦱

Maya

Ha! Definitely not the fun kind.
Notice: Maya and Leo use the present perfect to talk about experiences at any point in their lives — not a specific finished time. Words like ever, never, already, and yet often signal this use. We'll break down the forms next.
