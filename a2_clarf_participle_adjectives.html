The Woman Wearing a Red Coat — Quick Reference

Using a verb-ing phrase to say which person you mean

The idea: when there are many people, we say which one we mean by putting a verb-ing phrase straight after the noun: the woman wearing a red coat. The phrase works like an adjective — it describes the person — but it comes after the noun.
two sentences → one
The man is my uncle. He is carrying a suitcase. ↓ take out He is The man carrying a suitcase is my uncle.
the short way to say “who is …ing”
The girl who is holding a guitar is Mia. ↓ take out who is The girl holding a guitar is Mia.
Key point: noun + verb-ing …. There is no is and no who inside the phrase. The main verb of the sentence comes later: The woman sitting by the window is my boss.
Common mistakes
✗The man is carrying a suitcase is my uncle.
✓The man carrying a suitcase is my uncle. (no “is” inside the phrase)
✗The wearing a hat woman is my boss.
✓The woman wearing a hat is my boss. (the phrase goes after the noun)
✗The girl wear a red dress is Ana.
✓The girl wearing a red dress is Ana. (the verb needs -ing)
✗The boy who sitting on the chair is Sam.
✓The boy sitting on the chair is Sam. (or: who is sitting — never “who sitting”)

The -ing phrase always follows the person it describes, in statements, negatives and questions. It does an adjective's job, so the sentence still needs its own verb (is, isn't, know…).
Other positions
As the subject: The man riding the bicycle is my dad.
As the object: I know the woman standing next to the door.
In a question: Who is the boy carrying the pizza?  •  Is the girl wearing glasses your sister?
Two phrases: She's the woman wearing sunglasses and holding a cup.
