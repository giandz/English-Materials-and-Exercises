Describing a City — Clarification

Some city words you can count. Others you cannot.

Some nouns are count nouns — you can count them, and they have a plural: one park, two parks. Others are noncount nouns — you cannot count them and they have no plural: traffic, never traffics.
The two groups
Count 🔢
🌳 a park → two parks
🍽️ a restaurant → two restaurants
🏛️ a museum → two museums
🛍️ a shop → two shops
🏫 a school → two schools
Noncount 🚫
🚗 traffic
🚨 crime
🏭 pollution
🔊 noise
🗑️ litter
Which words go with which

There are a lot of parks. → count, plural

There is a lot of traffic. → noncount, singular

There are some shops. / There is some noise.

There are not many museums. → count

There is not much crime. → noncount

many + count nouns · much + noncount nouns · a lot of works with both. In questions: How many parks? but How much traffic?
Common mistakes
✗There are a lot of traffic.
✓There is a lot of traffic.
✗There isn't many crime.
✓There isn't much crime.
✗How much parks are there?
✓How many parks are there?
✗There are much restaurants.
✓There are many restaurants.
✗My city has a lot of pollutions.
✓My city has a lot of pollution.
