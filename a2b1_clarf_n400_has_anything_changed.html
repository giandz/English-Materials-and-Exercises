The news → present perfect: have / has + past participle. I’ve moved. I’ve changed jobs. My wife has had a baby. We don’t say when.

The details → past simple: I moved in May. I left on August 4th. When we say when, we use the past simple.

The question: Has anything changed since you filed? · Have you traveled since January? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t.

No changes? No, nothing has changed. Say it only when it is true.

American English: get → got → gotten. Have you gotten married?

Past participles you need
Tell the officer, and show a paper

Always tell the officer about a change, even a small one. Start like this: “I’d like to tell you about a change.” or “Two things have changed.”

Bring a paper that shows the change, and say: “Here is …”

After the interview

On the day of the oath ceremony, you answer the same kind of questions on a form: “Since your interview, have you traveled outside the United States? Have you married, or been divorced?” Answer them in the same way.
